AutoEvolve V2
Operating, Infrastructure & Go-to-Market Direction
Current direction after architecture review, 24/7 operating-model design, hardware sizing, API-cost planning, and Foundry integration
## 1. Executive direction
AutoEvolve is not just a collection of sales and marketing features. It should operate as a 24/7 commercial operating system: continuously researching markets, identifying companies, qualifying opportunities, monitoring inbound signals, preparing outreach and content, tracking replies and meetings, and surfacing only high-value decisions to the human operator.
The commercial role of AutoEvolve is dual-purpose:
Product: deploy AutoEvolve for B2B companies as a private sales and marketing operating system.
Internal engine: use AutoEvolve to acquire customers for AutoEvolve itself and for Foundry.
Operating principle: AutoEvolve runs continuously; humans work in review windows. High-impact actions remain human-approved until the system has sufficient evidence and controls to justify more autonomy.
## 2. Product scope and current readiness
Current application architecture already supports the main commercial loop: intake, company research, contact resolution, enrichment, AI-assisted drafts, approval, sending, delivery/reply webhooks, meetings/follow-up/suppression, campaign generation, media assembly, publishing handoff, attribution, and operational history.

## 3. AutoEvolve V2 infrastructure
The target V2 deployment is intentionally simple. The hardware should not become the bottleneck or the project. Provider/data spend and commercial execution matter more than infrastructure at the current stage.
## 3.1 Core node
Main VPS: 4 vCPU, 8 GB RAM, 80-120 GB SSD.
No GPU required for the default API-first deployment.
FastAPI application behind Cloudflare Tunnel / HTTPS / optional Zero Trust.
One application process while SQLite is the primary database.
Mounted persistence for data/, projects/, and config/.
Docker Compose or native system service are both valid deployment paths.
## 3.2 Optional compute
Media worker: separate 4 vCPU / 8 GB node only when FFmpeg/media rendering creates contention.
Local-model node: 16-32 GB RAM if Ollama becomes strategically useful; not required initially.
Future scale path: Postgres + separate queue/worker layer only after single-node concurrency or multi-instance needs justify it.
## 3.3 Security and entry path
Public/inbound signals -> Cloudflare/HTTPS -> FastAPI entrypoint -> authentication/validation/rate limits -> application services -> persistence/providers
Dashboard/operator routes require authentication.
Mutating/high-impact actions require an additional action gate/token where applicable.
Webhook and intake routes use their own secrets/signatures and request validation.
Provider/model endpoints must not be exposed unauthenticated to the public internet.
Secrets stay outside source control and are rotated if exposed.
## 4. 24/7 operating model
The weekly “gym split” remains useful for the human operator, but not for the machine. AutoEvolve should not stop researching on Tuesday because Monday was “push day.” The system runs continuously and separates work by execution risk and cost.
| TABLE |
| Mode | Runs when | Examples | Human involvement |
| Always-on metabolism | Continuous / event-driven | Forms, inbound email, reply/bounce monitoring, company-signal monitoring, lead scoring, provider health, analytics | Only on exception or high-value event |
| Scheduled training batches | Hourly / several times daily / daily | Deep research, enrichment, draft generation, follow-up generation, content packaging | Review selected output |
| Human gate | As surfaced by priority queue | Outbound send, publishing, LinkedIn actions, ambiguous replies, meetings, strategic changes | Explicit approve/edit/reject |
## 5. Commercial loops
## 5.1 Sales loop
Discover -> Qualify -> Enrich -> Draft -> Approve -> Send -> Reply -> Meeting -> Proposal -> Customer
Discover companies before contacts.
Use free/cheap public research to decide whether a company deserves paid enrichment.
Resolve one relevant decision-maker rather than many contacts by default.
Cache company/contact intelligence and reuse it.
Create grounded outreach from saved research rather than generic personalization.
Replies, bounces, meetings and suppression state stop inappropriate follow-up sequences.
## 5.2 Marketing loop
Research + sales objections -> content brief -> validated claims -> assets -> human approval -> Buffer draft -> publish -> tracked lead
Marketing is generated from real market research and sales conversations, not an isolated content calendar.
G1 remains the knowledge/claim control layer.
G2 handles media acquisition, generation and rendering.
G3 hands off publish-ready drafts and tracked URLs; final publishing remains reviewed.
Inbound leads preserve campaign/post attribution so content can be evaluated by pipeline contribution.
## 6. Two distinct acquisition programs
## 6.1 AutoEvolve customer program
Target: B2B companies roughly 5-100 employees.
Best signal: existing outbound/marketing activity combined with fragmented tooling and manual workflow.
Typical buyers: founder, Head of Sales, RevOps, Growth, operations-oriented owner.
Examples: agencies, consultancies, SaaS, recruiting, logistics, technical B2B services.
Research intensity: broad market monitoring, moderate-depth company qualification.
## 6.2 Foundry buyer program
Target: AI/software companies for whom verified engineering output is itself a business input.
Examples: coding-agent companies, developer tooling, code intelligence/evaluation, AI training/data, autonomous engineering systems.
Research intensity: lower volume, much deeper company and use-case intelligence.
Positioning: independently reviewed and gated engineering output, not merely “generated repositories.”
Goal: a small number of repeat high-value buyers, not mass-market outreach.
## 7. Lead-data and API strategy
Cost rule: never spend a paid lead/enrichment credit before the company has survived cheap qualification unless the signal itself requires paid data.
Recommended waterfall
1. Discover company from market/public sources.
2. Pull website, positioning, jobs, news and other cheap signals.
3. Score fit against the correct program ICP.
4. Stop if poor fit.
5. Use Apollo/company data only for qualified accounts where it adds value.
6. Resolve one relevant decision-maker.
7. Reuse cached contact/company intelligence where available.
8. Use Hunter for finder/verification or as a secondary layer.
9. Call additional paid providers only as fallbacks.
10. Store provider cost against the opportunity so unit economics are measurable.

## 11. Progressive overload and autonomy
Volume and autonomy increase only when quality metrics stay healthy. The gym analogy maps directly to system scaling: never add weight while form is breaking.
| TABLE |
| Phase | System behavior | Human review |
| Phase 1 - calibration | 20-40 company research/day, low-volume outreach, deep logging | Review almost every outbound action |
| Phase 2 - proven pattern | Increase qualified volume, automate more scoring/batching | Review outbound sends, exceptions, replies |
| Phase 3 - mature operation | 24/7 market radar, multiple programs, more automatic routine handling | Focus on high-value decisions and anomalies |
## 12. Immediate implementation plan
Assumption: remaining product fixing is approximately 1-2 focused engineering days. The objective is to move directly from “feature-complete enough” to “used internally every day.”
## Day 1 - deployment hardening
Run the full deployment-readiness audit against the actual repository and runtime.
Fix any P0/P1 issues found in authentication, webhooks, persistence, send idempotency, backup/restore, or clean-install reproducibility.
Deploy one internal AutoEvolve instance on the target VPS configuration.
Connect model gateway, Resend/email flow, Apollo/Hunter or chosen lead providers, and forms.
Verify restart persistence and restore from backup.
## Day 2 - commercial operating loop
Create two separate programs: AutoEvolve ICP and Foundry ICP.
Implement/confirm background schedules for research, scoring, enrichment batches, follow-ups and daily task generation.
Implement the operator priority card/queue.
Wire reply/meeting notifications.
Run first controlled prospect batch and track provider cost per opportunity.
Generate first marketing brief from real research/objections rather than a generic content prompt.
## Week 1 - calibration
Use AutoEvolve internally every day.
Collect delivery, reply, positive-reply, meeting and cost data.
Do not optimize volume yet.
Record manual steps that are repetitive and safe candidates for automation.
Turn the internal use case into proof for selling AutoEvolve deployments.
## 13. Definition of done for V2
AutoEvolve V2 is considered operationally complete for this stage when all of the following are true:
Fresh deployment on a clean VPS can be completed from documentation.
Application survives restart without losing critical state.
Backup and restore are tested, not merely configured.
At least one lead provider and one AI provider work end-to-end.
Sales loop completes from research through reply/meeting tracking.
Marketing loop completes from brief through publish handoff and attribution.
Forms and inbound events become prioritized leads.
High-impact actions cannot bypass required approvals.
Provider failures are visible and isolated rather than crashing unrelated features.
The operator receives a usable daily priority queue.
AutoEvolve and Foundry run as separate ICP/program configurations inside the same operating system.
Cost per qualified opportunity can be measured.
The system is used internally as the acquisition engine before scale is attempted.
## 14. What not to build yet
Do not migrate away from SQLite solely for architectural aesthetics. Migrate when concurrency/availability actually requires it.
Do not add a GPU unless local inference has a measurable cost/privacy/latency advantage.
Do not enrich every discovered contact.
Do not fully automate LinkedIn account actions just because they are technically possible.
Do not add many providers without a clear waterfall role.
Do not let self-improvement rewrite ICP, claims or messaging from tiny samples.
Do not confuse content volume with pipeline creation.
Do not create separate AutoEvolve deployments for AutoEvolve and Foundry; keep them as separate programs unless isolation becomes necessary.
